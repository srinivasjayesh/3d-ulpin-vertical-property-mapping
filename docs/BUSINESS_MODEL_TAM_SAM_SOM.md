# TAM / SAM / SOM — v2, Grounded in a Real Comparable Company

## Who buys and why

Likely buyers are municipal GIS, property-tax, planning and infrastructure departments, usually through an IT/procurement office or systems integrator. They pay for a governed map that links parcels, buildings and floors, reduces inspection preparation and makes data provenance visible. ROI must be measured with local baselines; this prototype does not calculate tax uplift or prove illegal construction.

Named public-sector references for the comparable market include BMC, Hubballi-Dharwad Municipal Corporation and Pune Municipal Corporation (as named end user). Genesys filings describe separate project awards/announcements; they do not establish recurring subscription revenue.

## Comparable and competition

Genesys International Corporation is the main comparable. Its March 2024 BMC release reports a ₹155.85 crore cover value (rounded to ₹155 crore in the body) for a 3D city-model/map-stack programme with three years of upkeep; consortium share and recurring allocation are not disclosed. Its March 2025 HDMC release reports ₹21.45 crore for 11 zones. Pune releases report ₹35 crore for a digital-twin/ICCC collaboration and a separate ₹15 crore regional-authority announcement; these are not one ₹50 crore contract. A Dharavi release reports ₹22 crore ex-GST awarded by Porter House; it is not a municipal award in that release.

Other competition includes Esri and GIS systems integrators, specialist property-survey firms, and internal QGIS/PostGIS teams. Positioning is a vertical identity/provenance layer that can complement a large 3D-city programme, not a claim to replace Genesys.

## Grounded sizing (INR)

These are planning scenarios, not published market facts. As a transparent proxy, use the 500-city AMRUT-I cohort: 500 cities × ₹20–40 lakh annual platform/support = **TAM ₹100–200 crore per year**. A one-time deployment at ₹50 lakh–₹1 crore per city is **₹250–500 crore across rollout**, not ARR.

For an initial serviceable segment, assume 50 cities pass geography, data-readiness and procurement filters: **SAM ₹10–20 crore annual**, plus ₹25–50 crore one-time deployment. The 50-city filter is a planning assumption and must be validated against tenders and budgets.

An obtainable first 18–24 month scenario is 2–4 limited-ward pilots. At ₹15–35 lakh deployment plus ₹6–12 lakh first-year licence/support, that is **₹21–47 lakh per contract** and **₹42 lakh–₹1.88 crore aggregate**. This is a target range, not a pipeline forecast; signed bookings, recognised revenue and cash are different.

## Margins

Planning assumptions: project gross margin 25–40%; recurring gross margin 60–75% after hosting, support and data costs. Example: ₹25 lakh project with ₹18 lakh direct cost = 28%; ₹8 lakh annual service with ₹3 lakh direct cost = 62.5%. These exclude sales, R&D, G&A, financing and tax. Genesys reported FY2025–26 consolidated EBITDA margin was about 39.36% and PAT margin about 10.01%; those are a diversified public company, not a promise for this product.
